# Ventilation and Heat Stress Management

Ventilation in a poultry house solves 5 critical objectives: providing oxygen, exhausting carbon dioxide, eliminating toxic ammonia, removing excess humidity, and cooling the flock. In Uzbekistan's intense climate, correct ventilation is a life-or-death factor for broilers beyond 25 days of age.

---

## 1. Minimum Ventilation (First 2–3 Weeks)
During early brooding, the primary goal is refreshing air without exhausting valuable heat or creating floor-level drafts.
* **Principle:** Incoming cold outdoor air must enter through high ceiling inlets, mix with upper thermal air layers, and gently descend warmed toward chick height.
* **Air Velocity:** At chick level, air speed must never exceed **0.15–0.2 m/s** during the first 2 weeks.
* **Drafts Kill:** Cold drafts flowing directly along the floor chill chicks, causing corner pile-ups and suffocation.

---

## 2. Ammonia Hazards ($NH_3$) and Damp Litter
Ammonia is generated by bacterial fermentation of uric acid in damp litter.
* **Farmer's Field Test:** If entering the barn burns your eyes or causes a sharp nasal sting, ammonia exceeds 20 ppm!
* For broilers, this is catastrophic: ammonia strips tracheal mucosal cilia, enabling E. coli and mycoplasma to penetrate deep into air sacs, causing chronic respiratory complexes.
* **Action:** Immediately increase fresh air intake and replace damp litter spots with fresh dry shavings.

---

## 3. Tunnel Ventilation and Heat Stress (After Day 25)
Broilers over 4 weeks weigh > 1.5 kg. Birds possess no sweat glands, and dense plumage retains body heat. Their sole cooling mechanisms are panting (respiratory evaporative loss) and convective heat transfer from airspeed.
* **Tunnel Effect:** Large exhaust fans extract air from one end of the barn, pulling a longitudinal wind stream at **2.0–2.5 m/s**.
* At this airspeed, convective heat loss produces a chill effect making birds perceive temperatures 4–6°C lower than actual ambient air.

---

## 4. Evaporative Cooling Pads and Fogging
When outdoor temperatures in Uzbekistan climb above 35–38°C, fan circulation alone is insufficient (hot airflow acts like a hairdryer):
* **Cooling Pads:** Water flows over cellulose paper pads at air inlets. Hot dry outside air passes through, evaporates moisture, and drops 6–10°C in temperature.
* **Golden Rule:** Activate pad water ONLY when relative humidity is below 70% and temperature is above 28°C. If barn air is already humid, evaporation stops and suffocating heat stroke occurs.

---

## 5. Practical Hot-Weather Countermeasures for Farmers:
1. **Roof Whitewashing:** Whitewash outer roof surfaces with lime — reflected solar radiation reduces interior barn heat by 3–4°C.
2. **Night Feeding Shift:** During peak heat (12:00 to 17:00), elevate feed lines or dim lighting. Digestive heat increment triggers fatal heat stroke. Feed birds during cool night and early dawn hours.
3. **Flushing Water Lines:** Keep drinker lines cool (18–20°C). Regularly flush stagnated, hot pipe water during peak afternoon hours.
4. **Vitamin C & Electrolytes:** Provide sodium bicarbonate, potassium chloride, and Vitamin C in drinking water 2–3 days prior to forecasted heatwaves.
