# Temperature and Humidity Standards

Day-old broiler chicks cannot regulate their internal body temperature. During the first 10–14 days, thermoregulation depends entirely on the housing microclimate. An error of just 2–3 degrees Celsius can trigger sudden mortality or permanent growth stunting.

---

## 1. Broiler Temperature and Humidity Schedule

Measurements are taken strictly at **chick back level** (5–8 cm from the floor), never at the ceiling!

| Bird Age | Air Temperature (°C) | Bedding Temperature (°C) | Relative Humidity (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day 1** | **33 – 34°C** | 30 – 32°C | 60 – 70% |
| **Day 3** | **33°C** | 30°C | 60 – 70% |
| **Day 7 (Week 1)** | **31 – 32°C** | 29 – 30°C | 60 – 65% |
| **Day 14 (Week 2)** | **28 – 29°C** | 27 – 28°C | 60 – 65% |
| **Day 21 (Week 3)** | **26 – 27°C** | 25 – 26°C | 55 – 60% |
| **Day 28 (Week 4)** | **23 – 24°C** | 22 – 23°C | 55 – 60% |
| **Day 35 (Week 5)** | **21 – 22°C** | 20 – 21°C | 55 – 65% |
| **Day 42 (Week 6)** | **19 – 20°C** | 19 – 20°C | 55 – 65% |

---

## 2. Bedding Temperature Outweighs Air Temperature
Why floor temperature is vital:
* Chicks rest their feet and bellies on the litter 100% of the time.
* If house air registers 34°C but concrete flooring under thin litter sits at 22°C, chicks suffer hypothermia within 3–4 hours.
* Chilled footpads drain core blood flow, inhibiting yolk sac absorption and inciting fatal yolk sac peritonitis (primary cause of mortality on Days 3–5).
* **Field Rule:** Touch a chick's footpad to your cheek: the chick's foot must feel pleasantly warm! If cold, litter has not preheated properly.

---

## 3. Relative Humidity (RH)
* **Week 1 (60–70%):** Hatcheries maintain high humidity (70–80%). If barn air is dry (< 50%), chicks lose pulmonary moisture rapidly, down parches, and respiratory tracts inflame. Humidify walkways or mist air to preserve humidity.
* **From Week 2 Onward (55–65%):** High humidity (> 70%) combined with warmth (25–28°C) is hazardous — litter cakes, ammonia spikes, and coccidia proliferate.

---

## 4. Managing Diurnal Temperature Fluctuations
In Uzbekistan's continental climate, day-night temperatures often swing by 15–20°C (e.g. +25°C daytime down to +5°C night).
* **Critical Mistake:** Shutting off heat overnight or leaving ventilation shutters wide open during night cold snaps.
* Diurnal fluctuations inside the poultry barn should never exceed **1.5–2°C**.
* Severe swings trigger viral respiratory complexes (IB, mycoplasma) and metabolic stress that halts weight gain.

---

## 5. Sensor and Thermometer Placement
* Install sensors strictly at chick head/back level.
* Maintain at least 3 checkpoints: under the brooder/heater, near perimeter walls, and beside drinker lines.
* Shield sensors from radiant infrared lamps to avoid falsely elevated readings.
