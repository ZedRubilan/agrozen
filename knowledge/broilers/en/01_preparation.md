# Barn Preparation Prior to Chick Placement

Over 70% of broiler farming success is determined by how thoroughly the house is prepared before day-old chicks arrive. Placing chicks in damp, cold, or inadequately sanitized housing leads to heavy mortality within the first 10 days.

---

## 1. Mechanical Cleaning and Washing
Preparation begins immediately after depopulating the previous batch:
1. **Litter Removal:** Completely transport all old litter and manure at least 50 meters away from the poultry house.
2. **Dry Cleaning:** Sweep dust, cobwebs, and down from ceilings, trusses, light fixtures, and fans. Dust harbors enormous bacterial and viral loads.
3. **High-Pressure Washing:** Wash the entire house with high-pressure water (minimum 100–140 bar). Use a 1–2% detergent or washing soda solution to dissolve fatty residues, paying meticulous attention to wall-floor seams and corners.

---

## 2. Disinfection and Equipment Sanitation
Disinfection is only effective on clean, fully dried surfaces:
* **Wall and Ceiling Whitewashing:** Treat walls with a 20% fresh slaked lime suspension combined with 2–3% copper sulfate to eradicate fungi and bacteria.
* **Wet Disinfection:** Apply certified broad-spectrum disinfectants (glutaraldehyde, quaternary ammonium, or Virocid formulations) following manufacturer specifications.
* **Equipment Care:** Soak feeders, drinkers, and hoses in disinfectant solution, rinse thoroughly with fresh water, and sun-dry.
* **Sanitary Downtime ("All-In / All-Out"):** After disinfection, keep the barn sealed and vacant for at least **4–7 days**.

---

## 3. Pest Exclusion and Wild Bird Biosecurity
Rodents carry Salmonella and Pasteurella, while wild birds vector Avian Influenza and Newcastle Disease:
* Mesh all windows, eaves, and ventilation inlets with galvanized wire (aperture ≤ 1.5 cm).
* Seal all foundation and wall crevices using mortar reinforced with crushed glass.
* Set tamper-proof rodent bait stations along the exterior building perimeter.

---

## 4. Bedding: Selection and Depth
Broilers spend their entire life cycle on bedding. Cold or wet litter induces footpad dermatitis, ascites, and systemic infection.
* **Material:** Ideal choices are dust-free, dry, medium-to-coarse wood shavings (conifer or hardwood) or clean chopped wheat straw.
* **Layer Depth:** Winter and early spring: **7–10 cm**; Summer: **5–7 cm**.
* **Requirements:** Bedding must be completely dry (moisture < 15%), mold-free, and odorless. Avoid fine sawdust; chicks ingest it, causing crop impaction.

---

## 5. Barn Pre-Heating (24–48 Hours Ahead)
A common novice mistake is activating heating just 2 hours before arrival. Air warms quickly, but concrete floors remain freezing cold.
* **24–48 Hours Prior:** Ignite heaters (gas brooders, space heaters, radiant tubes).
* **Floor Temperature:** At placement, bedding surface temperature must measure **30–32°C**, and air temperature at chick height must be **33–34°C**.
* If a chick sits on cold flooring, internal organs chill, yolk sac absorption fails, and systemic sepsis follows.

---

## 6. Pre-Placement Readiness Checklist
Before dispatching transport to pick up chicks, verify:
- [ ] Heating operates stably with bedding temperature at 30–32°C.
- [ ] Drinkers are filled with fresh water at room temperature (20–22°C).
- [ ] Starter crumb feed is distributed on paper brooding sheets and initial feeder pans.
- [ ] Lighting is set to full intensity (30–40 lux).
- [ ] Minimum ventilation is tuned with zero floor drafts.
- [ ] Calibrated thermometers and hygrometers are set at chick eye level (5–8 cm above floor).
- [ ] Footwear disinfection mat is prepared at the entrance.
