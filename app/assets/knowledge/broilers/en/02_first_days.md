# Chick Placement and the First 3 Days

The first 72 hours of a broiler chick's life directly determine harvest weight. If chicks fail to quadruple their initial hatch weight by Day 7 (target: ≥ 180 g), this growth deficit cannot be recovered by late feeding or medications.

---

## 1. Chick Unloading and Quality Assessment
* **Unloading Speed:** Unload chick boxes promptly without rough movements. Never drop boxes to the floor.
* **Initial Culling:** Inspect batch quality upon delivery. A healthy day-old chick:
  * Weighs between 40–44 grams;
  * Stands firmly and alertly on its legs with bright, clean eyes;
  * Has a fully healed, dry navel free from scabs and bleeding;
  * Possesses a soft, pliable abdomen without bloating;
  * Has clean, dry, fluffy down with no adhering shell fragments.
* Isolate weak or deformed chicks immediately under dedicated warmth or cull humanely.

---

## 2. First 2 Hours: Water Only!
Chicks arriving from hatchery transit are severely dehydrated. **Water takes absolute priority over feed!**
* **Water Temperature:** Strictly maintained at **20–22°C**. Freezing water causes visceral hypothermia and gut cramping, while water above 25°C rapidly cultivates bacteria.
* **First Drink:** Adding glucose (20–30 g per liter) or vitamin-electrolyte stress packs assists rapid transport recovery.
* **Guiding to Drinkers:** Gently dip the beaks of a few chicks into drinkers — flock peers rapidly mimic them.
* Drinker availability: 1 bell drinker per 50 chicks or 1 nipple per 10–12 birds.

---

## 3. Feed Presentation (2 Hours Post-Watering)
Only after the entire flock has drunk water should feed be introduced.
* **Brooding Paper:** Spread dedicated textured brooding paper covering at least 20–30% of the brooding area (do not use ink newsprint as newsprint ink is toxic).
* **Paper Rustle:** Chicks running over the paper generate rustling sounds, naturally drawing peers to explore feed crumbs.
* **Feed Texture:** Use only high-quality starter crumbs (1.5–2 mm particle size). Powdery flour-like feed obstructs nostrils and beaks.
* Replenish feed frequently in modest amounts (5–6 times daily) to preserve aroma and cleanliness.

---

## 4. Lighting Regimen in Early Days
* **Days 1–3:** 23 hours of bright light (30–40 lux) with 1 hour of total darkness.
* The 1 hour of darkness conditions chicks to darkness, preventing catastrophic piling and smothering during sudden power outages.

---

## 5. The Crop Fill Test (Primary Health Metric)
This is the single most objective field test of placement quality:
* **8 Hours Post-Placement:** Sample 50 chicks from varied zones. At least **80%** must have soft, pliable crops filled with blended feed and water (like a moist sponge).
* **24 Hours Post-Placement:** At least **95–100%** must display full crops.
* *Hard crop:* Chicks found feed but lack water (water too cold or lines too high).
* *Watery crop:* Chicks drinking but failing to find feed (insufficient feeder space or inadequate light).
* *Empty crop:* Chicks chilled or experiencing severe stress.

---

## 6. Reading Chick Behavior for Climate Assessment
Thermometers can misread, but chicks never lie:
* **Ideal:** Chicks evenly dispersed across the brooding floor, eating, drinking, or resting stretched out. Soft, contented chirping.
* **Cold:** Huddled in dense clusters directly beneath heaters, chirping distressfully. High risk of bottom birds smothering.
* **Hot:** Fleeing heat sources toward perimeter walls, panting with open beaks and outstretched wings. Feed intake ceases.
* **Drafts:** Clustered against one corner away from cold incoming air currents.
